## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML/CSS/JS (user's choice, 2026-09-28). No framework, no build step for the page itself. Deployable files live in `site/`; everything else in the project root is development-only.

## Users

People in Wrocław (the shop is at Wyszyńskiego 102, Ołbin/Nadodrze side of the centre) with something worn or broken that is worth saving: shoes that hurt or do not fit, a bag or backpack with a dead zipper, a suitcase, a key to copy, a chair or armchair that needs new upholstery, a car seat, a medical couch. Many are Polish, many are Ukrainian; the workshop is run by craftsmen from Ukraine, so the site is bilingual (Polish default, Ukrainian switch).

## Product Purpose

The website of Angel But, a small repair workshop: shoemaking and upholstery under one roof, plus leather goods, tailoring, key cutting, tool sharpening and suitcase repair. It must make clear, within seconds, that this one place fixes all of these, show real work, and get the visitor to call or text a photo of their item for a quote.

## Positioning

A neighbourhood workshop that saves things instead of replacing them, with an unusually wide set of trades in one small shop (the shop sign lists seven). Reviews praise: shoes that finally fit or stop hurting, bags restored with sentimental value intact, quick jobs (one review: shoes done in two days), fair prices, craftsmen who know their trade. The name and logo (a boot with angel wings) come from the owner's own sign.

## Product Principles

1. Show the work: real photos of real repairs beat any slogan.
2. One shop, many trades: the breadth is the story, but each trade must be findable in seconds.
3. Two languages, one voice: Polish and Ukrainian carry the same content.
4. Promise only what is checkable; let customers' own words carry the praise.
